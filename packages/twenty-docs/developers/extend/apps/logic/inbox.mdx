## Overview

Your app can start and continue conversations in the inbox of workspace members, where they also talk with Twenty's assistant. A message can end on a question, a form or a change to approve, which the member answers from the chat before the conversation continues with the assistant.

The inbox helpers are exported from both `twenty-sdk/logic-function` and `twenty-sdk/front-component`, so a scheduled run and a button in your app post the same way.

## Sending a message

`sendInboxMessage()` posts a message from your app in the chats of one or more workspace members, in a channel or in a conversation your app may write to, and returns the conversation's `threadId`, the `messageId`, whether the conversation is new (`isNewThread`) and whether a member had deleted it (`isDismissed`), in which case nothing was written.

```ts src/logic-functions/notify-first-recording.ts
import { sendInboxMessage } from 'twenty-sdk/logic-function';

const { threadId } = await sendInboxMessage({
  workspaceMemberIds: [workspaceMemberId],
  threadKey: 'first-call-recording',
  idempotencyKey: 'first-call-recording',
  title: 'Your first call recording is ready',
  text: 'Your first call was recorded: **Weekly sync**.',
  toolCall: {
    toolName: 'ask_question',
    input: {
      header: 'Share',
      question: 'Do you want to share it with the other attendees?',
      options: [{ label: 'Draft a recap email' }, { label: 'Not now' }],
    },
  },
});
```

Key points:

- `workspaceMemberIds` lists the members who receive the conversation. The first member of a new conversation owns it, and the others follow it as participants. A later send under the same key that lists new members adds them; members it leaves out keep the conversation. Recipients find the conversation unread in their inbox, with no obligation to answer: [mention](#mentioning-members) the members you need something from.
- `threadKey` picks the conversation for this app, shared by every member it is sent to. A new key starts a conversation titled `title`; a key you used before adds the message to that conversation. To write in a conversation your app did not open, pass its [`threadId`](#replying-in-an-existing-conversation) instead.
- `channelId` (optional, with `threadKey`) files a new conversation in a channel: see [posting in a channel](#posting-in-a-channel). A conversation that exists stays where it is.
- `idempotencyKey` identifies the message within the conversation. Sending again with the same keys writes nothing new, so retries and concurrent runs never duplicate a message.
- `text` is markdown, and can carry [mentions and record references](#mentions-and-record-references).
- `recordTargets` (optional) lists records to [attach](#attaching-records-to-a-conversation) to the conversation before the message is written, each as `objectNameSingular` and `recordId`. A record your app cannot read fails the call with `RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, and nothing is written.
- `toolCall` (optional) ends the message on one of:
  - a call the member answers, which pauses the conversation: `ask_question` (one multiple-choice question), `request_form` (fields to fill in) or `propose_tool_call` (a call to approve). Its `input` takes the `toolName`, the `arguments` you would call the tool with and a one-sentence `summary`. The tool must be one your app's default role could run itself, such as `create_one_person` or `update_one_opportunity` (with the record `id`); an email (`send_email` or `draft_email`, with `recipients`, `subject` and a `body` in HTML) needs no permission of your app. The member reviews the call in the same card as a call the assistant proposes, edits it if they want, and the approved call runs with their own access;
  - one of your app's own tools, by `logicFunctionUniversalIdentifier`, rendered by its front component with the `input` and `output` you pass. It does not pause the conversation.
- Only one call the member answers can wait in a conversation at a time: sending another before they answer fails with `THREAD_AWAITING_ANSWER`. Plain messages and app tool calls are still accepted.
- Only the member who owns a conversation can delete it, and a deleted conversation is gone for every member: it is not recreated, and nothing is added to it. The other members can unsubscribe to take it out of their own inbox.
- Every member must have the `AI` permission, and the call fails without writing the message when one of them cannot have the conversation. Your app's [default role](/developers/extend/apps/config/roles) must grant `SystemPermissionFlag.AI`.
- A message needs somewhere to go: a recipient, a mentioned member, a channel or an existing conversation. A call that names none, or that gives both `threadKey` and `threadId`, fails with `INVALID_INBOX_MESSAGE_INPUT`.

### Who sends the message

A message is always written by your app, whatever triggered the call. The call itself runs like the default [API clients](/developers/extend/apps/logic/logic-functions#whose-access-a-call-uses):

- In a logic function, it acts as the person who triggered the run, or as your application when nobody did, such as on a cron schedule.
- In a front component, it acts as the member looking at the page. When the token has expired, the call refreshes it once through Twenty and retries.

The person behind the call needs the `AI` permission as well, like every recipient.

To post from a front component, import the same helpers from `twenty-sdk/front-component`. [Posting to the inbox](/developers/extend/apps/layout/front-components#posting-to-the-inbox) shows how to open the conversation afterwards.

## Replying in an existing conversation

Pass `threadId` instead of `threadKey` to add a message to a conversation that already exists, such as one a member started, or one your app opened under a key you no longer have.

```ts src/logic-functions/post-transcript.ts
import { sendInboxMessage } from 'twenty-sdk/logic-function';

await sendInboxMessage({
  threadId,
  idempotencyKey: `transcript-${call.id}`,
  text: `The transcript of **${call.title}** is ready.`,
});
```

- The call runs with the same access as [sending a message](#who-sends-the-message): a member must be able to edit the conversation, and your application can write in a conversation it wrote in itself or one its default role was granted; otherwise the call fails with `THREAD_NOT_FOUND`.
- `idempotencyKey` identifies the message within the conversation for your app: another app using the same key writes its own message.
- The conversation keeps its title, owner and channel, and no opening message is added: `channelId` is refused, and `title` is ignored.
- `workspaceMemberIds` and `mentionedWorkspaceMemberIds` add members to a conversation that has an owner, as they do for a conversation your app opened.

## Posting in a channel

Pass `channelId` with `threadKey` to file a new conversation in a channel, where every member whose role reaches the channel finds it in the channel's Open list.

```ts src/logic-functions/escalate-ticket.ts
import { sendInboxMessage } from 'twenty-sdk/logic-function';

const { threadId } = await sendInboxMessage({
  channelId: supportChannelId,
  threadKey: `ticket-${ticket.id}`,
  idempotencyKey: `ticket-${ticket.id}-opened`,
  title: `Ticket ${ticket.number}: ${ticket.subject}`,
  text: ticket.summary,
});
```

- Your app's default role must reach the channel: it must be one of the channel's roles, or have access to all channels. Otherwise the call fails with `CHAT_CHANNEL_NOT_FOUND`.
- Without `workspaceMemberIds`, the conversation has no owner and no member follows it: the channel's roles read it, and members join it from the channel. With recipients, the first one owns it, and must reach the channel too.
- `channelId` only applies when the conversation is created. A send under a key your app used before adds the message to that conversation, wherever it is filed.

## Mentioning members

`mentionedWorkspaceMemberIds` lists the members the message needs something from. A mentioned member follows the conversation like a recipient and finds it under Needs input until they answer or mark it read; a member who is only listed in `workspaceMemberIds` finds it unread, with nothing to answer.

```ts src/logic-functions/request-review.ts
import { inboxMention, sendInboxMessage } from 'twenty-sdk/logic-function';

const reviewerMention = await inboxMention({ workspaceMemberId: reviewer.id });

await sendInboxMessage({
  workspaceMemberIds: [owner.id, reviewer.id],
  mentionedWorkspaceMemberIds: [reviewer.id],
  threadKey: `quote-${quote.id}`,
  idempotencyKey: `quote-${quote.id}-review`,
  title: `Review the quote for ${quote.companyName}`,
  text: `${reviewerMention}, can you review this quote before it goes out?`,
});
```

- A mentioned member is added to the recipients, so they receive the conversation even when `workspaceMemberIds` leaves them out.
- The mention in `text` only renders: write it with [`inboxMention`](#mentions-and-record-references), and list the member in `mentionedWorkspaceMemberIds` to notify them.
- Sending the same message again, with the same keys, creates no new obligation.

## Mentions and record references

The inbox renders reference tokens in `text` as chips. Write them with these helpers rather than by hand:

```ts src/logic-functions/flag-idle-deal.ts
import {
  inboxMention,
  inboxReferenceRecord,
  sendInboxMessage,
} from 'twenty-sdk/logic-function';

const ownerMention = await inboxMention({ workspaceMemberId: owner.id });

const dealReference = await inboxReferenceRecord({
  objectNameSingular: 'opportunity',
  recordId: opportunity.id,
});

await sendInboxMessage({
  workspaceMemberIds: [owner.id],
  threadKey: `idle-deal-${opportunity.id}`,
  idempotencyKey: `idle-deal-${opportunity.id}-${weekNumber}`,
  title: `${opportunity.name} is idle`,
  text: `${ownerMention}, ${dealReference} has had no activity for two weeks.`,
});
```

- `inboxMention({ workspaceMemberId })` writes a mention of a workspace member, and `inboxReferenceRecord({ objectNameSingular, recordId })` a chip that links to any record.
- Both read the label the chip shows, as the composer does: the member's full name, or their email when the name is empty, and the record's label identifier, such as a company name. Brackets become parentheses and line breaks become spaces, so the chip always renders.
- They read with the same access as the rest of the call, so that access needs to reach the member or record. A record reference makes two requests, one for the object's label field and one for the record.
- A mention only renders. It does not add the member to the conversation or notify them: list everyone who should receive the message in `workspaceMemberIds`, and the members you need something from in [`mentionedWorkspaceMemberIds`](#mentioning-members).
- `INBOX_ASSISTANT_MENTION` is the text the composer writes when a member mentions the assistant (`@Ada`), so your messages name it the same way.

## Attaching records to a conversation

`sendInboxMessage()` attaches the records listed in `recordTargets` before it writes the message. `attachRecordToInboxThread()` links a conversation to a record afterwards, and `detachRecordFromInboxThread()` removes the link.

```ts src/logic-functions/file-call-recap.ts
import {
  attachRecordToInboxThread,
  sendInboxMessage,
} from 'twenty-sdk/logic-function';

const { threadId } = await sendInboxMessage({
  workspaceMemberIds: [owner.id],
  threadKey: `call-recap-${call.id}`,
  idempotencyKey: `call-recap-${call.id}`,
  title: `Recap of ${call.title}`,
  text: call.summary,
  recordTargets: [{ objectNameSingular: 'company', recordId: company.id }],
});

await attachRecordToInboxThread({
  threadId,
  objectNameSingular: 'person',
  recordId: attendee.id,
});
```

- Both take the conversation's `threadId` and the record as `objectNameSingular` and `recordId`. A conversation can be linked to people, companies, opportunities and custom object records.
- Both are idempotent: attaching a record twice keeps a single link, and detaching a record that is not attached changes nothing.
- The call runs with the same access as [sending a message](#who-sends-the-message), which needs the `AI` permission. A member must be able to edit the conversation, and your application can act on a conversation it wrote in itself or one its default role was granted; otherwise the call fails with `THREAD_NOT_FOUND`.
- The record must be readable with that access, or the call fails with `RECORD_NOT_FOUND`.
