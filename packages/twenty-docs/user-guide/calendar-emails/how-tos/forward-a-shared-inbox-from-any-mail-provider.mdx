---
title: Forward a Shared Inbox from Any Email Provider
description: Point a shared inbox at a Twenty email channel, whatever mail provider you use.
---

Twenty picks up shared-inbox mail by having your provider send a copy of every incoming message to a private address that Twenty generates for you. Set that up once and the inbox flows into your workspace.

Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace have enough quirks to deserve their own walkthroughs:

- [Microsoft 365](/user-guide/calendar-emails/how-tos/forward-a-shared-inbox-from-microsoft-365)
- [Google Workspace](/user-guide/calendar-emails/how-tos/forward-a-shared-inbox-from-google-workspace)

This page covers every other provider.

## Part 1: Create the channel in Twenty

1. Go to **Settings → Communication**.
2. Click **Add email channel**.

<img
  src="/images/user-guide/emails/inbound-channels/03-twenty-add-email-channel.png"
  style={{ width: '100%' }}
/>

3. In **Source Email Address**, type the shared address you want to receive mail at, for example `contact@yourcompany.com`.
4. In **Display Name**, type the name recipients see.
5. Click **Save**.

<img
  src="/images/user-guide/emails/inbound-channels/04-twenty-new-channel-form.png"
  style={{ width: '100%' }}
/>

6. Twenty now shows a **Forwarding address**. It ends in `@inbound.twenty.com`. Click **Copy**.

<img
  src="/images/user-guide/emails/inbound-channels/05-twenty-forwarding-address.png"
  style={{ width: '100%' }}
/>

<Note>
  Each channel gets its own forwarding address. Never point two mailboxes at the
  same one, or their mail will land in the same place.
</Note>

## Part 2: Pick the right kind of forwarding

Most providers offer more than one way to send mail onwards, and only one of them works properly.

Twenty reads the `From` header of whatever arrives to work out who wrote the message. That is how a message gets linked to the right person and company.

- **Server-side forwarding or redirecting** leaves the original sender in place. Your customer stays the sender, the message lands on their record, and replies go back to them. Some providers call this **Redirect**, others just call it **Forwarding**. This is the one you want.
- **Forwarding as an attachment**, or any rule that composes a brand new message, replaces the sender with your own address. Every conversation then looks like you wrote it, and nothing matches a contact.

So: use the automatic forwarding setting in your mail account or server. Do not build a rule that wraps the message up and sends it on as something new.

<Warning>
  Also avoid rules that strip attachments or convert messages to plain text.
  Twenty stores whatever it is handed.
</Warning>

Wherever you can, tick the option that **keeps a copy** in the original mailbox, so your team can still work the inbox normally.

## Part 4: Test it

Send an email from an outside address (a personal Gmail works) to `contact@yourcompany.com`. It should show up in Twenty within a minute or two.

## Troubleshooting

<Accordion title="Nothing arrives">
  Check the forwarding address character for character against the one in
  Twenty. A single wrong character means the mail goes nowhere and is silently
  dropped.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Mail arrives, but every message looks like it came from us">
  The rule is rebuilding the message instead of passing it along, so your
  address ends up in the `From` header. Switch to your provider's plain
  forwarding or redirect setting, and turn off any "forward as attachment"
  option.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The sender gets a bounce about forwarding not being allowed">
  Your provider blocks automatic forwarding to outside domains. Look for a
  security or anti-spam setting that allows external forwarding, and allow it
  for this mailbox only.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Mail disappeared from the original inbox">
  The rule moves messages on instead of copying them. Change it to keep a copy,
  or add a second rule that delivers to the inbox.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The channel receives mail but cannot send">
  Receiving and sending are separate. Sending needs your domain verified under
  the channel's **Sending domain** section, which means adding the DNS records
  Twenty lists there.
</Accordion>
