---
title: Email Channels
description: Share an address like contact@ with your team, reply from it, and send campaigns from its domain.
---

An email channel is an address your whole workspace shares, such as `contact@yourcompany.com`. You add it once in **Settings → Communication**, and every message it receives shows up on the right people and companies in Twenty.

A channel has up to three parts. Only the first one is required. Set up the others when you need what they unlock.

| You want to                            | Forwarding | Sending records | Unsubscribe records |
| -------------------------------------- | ---------- | --------------- | ------------------- |
| See the shared inbox in Twenty         | Required   | Not needed      | Not needed          |
| Reply from the shared address          | Required   | Required        | Not needed          |
| Send campaigns from the address domain | Optional   | Required        | Required            |

## Part 1: Receive mail with forwarding

Your mail provider sends a copy of every incoming message to a private forwarding address that Twenty gives you. No DNS changes are needed for this part.

Once forwarding is on, the channel works as a shared mailbox: your team reads the conversations in Twenty, linked to the people and companies they involve.

Follow the guide for your provider:

- [Microsoft 365](/user-guide/calendar-emails/how-tos/forward-a-shared-inbox-from-microsoft-365)
- [Google Workspace](/user-guide/calendar-emails/how-tos/forward-a-shared-inbox-from-google-workspace)
- [Any other provider](/user-guide/calendar-emails/how-tos/forward-a-shared-inbox-from-any-mail-provider)

## Part 2: Reply with sending records

To reply from the shared address, Twenty has to send on behalf of your domain. Mail providers only accept that once you prove you own the domain, with DNS records where your domain is managed:

- three `CNAME` records ending in `._domainkey`
- an `MX` and a `TXT` record on `twenty-bounce`

Twenty also suggests a DMARC record if your domain does not have one yet.

Open the channel in **Settings → Communication** and scroll to **Sending domain**. These records are listed under **Domain authentication**. When it shows **Verified**, your team can reply from the shared address.

<Note>
  If you only reply from the channel, you can skip the unsubscribe records. The
  **Unsubscribe link** row stays **Pending**, and that does not block replies.
</Note>

[Set up a sending domain](/user-guide/email-campaigns/how-tos/set-up-a-sending-domain) walks through adding and verifying each record at your DNS provider.

## Part 3: Send campaigns with unsubscribe records

Campaigns must include a working unsubscribe link, and Twenty hosts that page on your own domain, for example `unsubscribe.yourcompany.com`. That takes two more `CNAME` records, on `unsubscribe` and `_acme-challenge.unsubscribe`, listed under **Unsubscribe link** in the same **Sending domain** section.

Twenty refuses to send a campaign until both **Domain authentication** and **Unsubscribe link** show **Verified**.

To learn more, read [Email Campaigns](/user-guide/email-campaigns/overview), then [send your first campaign](/user-guide/email-campaigns/how-tos/send-your-first-campaign).
