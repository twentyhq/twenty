title: Email Campaign Best Practices
description: What to do, and what to avoid, so your campaigns reach the inbox and your domain stays in good standing.
---

Mailbox providers decide where your campaign lands based on who you send to and how they react. A campaign that goes to people who asked for it reaches the inbox. One sent to a bought or stale list lands in spam, and enough spam reports can pause sending from your domain.

This page covers what Twenty handles for you, what is up to you, and the numbers to keep an eye on.

## What Twenty handles for you

- **Authentication**: once your [sending domain](/user-guide/email-campaigns/how-tos/set-up-a-sending-domain) is verified, every campaign is signed with DKIM and passes SPF on your own domain.
- **Unsubscribe link**: every campaign gets an unsubscribe link in the footer and the one-click unsubscribe headers Gmail and Yahoo require of bulk senders. Opt-outs take effect immediately.
- **Suppression**: addresses that bounce, report spam or unsubscribe are skipped on every later campaign, with no action needed from you.
- **Duplicates**: each person is sent one copy, even if they appear twice in a list.

## What is up to you

Twenty cannot know how you collected your contacts or what your company's address is. These are yours to get right.

### Do

- **Only email people who asked to hear from you.** They signed up, bought from you or agreed to receive your emails. Keep a record of when and how they agreed.
- **Include your postal address.** US law (CAN-SPAM) requires a physical address in every marketing email. Add it to the bottom of your campaign.
- **Start small on a new domain.** Send your first campaigns to your most engaged contacts, and grow the volume over a few weeks. See [Warm up a new domain](#warm-up-a-new-domain).
- **Use a recognizable sender.** Keep the same **From** address and display name from one campaign to the next, so recipients know who you are.
- **Write a subject line that matches the content.** Misleading subject lines are illegal in many countries and drive spam reports everywhere.
- **Clean your lists.** Remove people who have not engaged with you, by replying, buying or signing in, in six to twelve months, rather than mailing them indefinitely.
- **Send a test first.** Check the layout, the links and the sender before every campaign.
- **Use unsubscribe topics.** Topics let people opt out of one kind of email, like a newsletter, instead of everything. See [Manage unsubscribes and topics](/user-guide/email-campaigns/how-tos/manage-unsubscribes-and-topics).

### Don't

- **Don't buy, rent or scrape lists.** Purchased lists are full of spam traps: addresses that exist only to catch senders who never had permission. A few hits are enough to damage your domain.
- **Don't import your whole CRM into a campaign list.** Twenty can create people from your synced inbox and calendar. Having exchanged an email with someone is not the same as their agreement to receive marketing.
- **Don't send one large blast from a new domain.** A sudden jump in volume looks like spam, even when the list is clean.
- **Don't build the email from a single image.** Emails that are mostly images and little text are more likely to be filtered. Put your message in real text.
- **Don't use public link shorteners**, like bit.ly. Spammers use them to hide where links lead, so filters treat them with suspicion.
- **Don't try to re-add people who unsubscribed or reported spam.** Twenty blocks them for a reason.

<Note>
  Lists of "spam words" to avoid in subject lines are mostly a myth. Modern
  filters care far more about your domain's reputation and how recipients react
  than about individual words.
</Note>

## Numbers to watch

After each campaign, open it and compare the **Bounced count** and **Complained count** with the **Sent count**.

| Measure             | Aim for    | Why it matters                                                                                                      |
| ------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Spam complaint rate | Below 0.1% | Gmail asks bulk senders to stay under 0.1% and never reach 0.3%. Above that, mail is rejected or sent to spam.      |
| Bounce rate         | Below 2%   | A high bounce rate means the list is out of date. At 5% or more, your email provider starts reviewing your sending. |

A complaint rate of 0.1% is one spam report per 1,000 emails. If you see more, stop and look at who you are sending to before your next campaign.

<Warning>
  If too many recipients bounce or mark your emails as spam, sending from your
  domain can be paused to protect everyone else's deliverability.
</Warning>

## Warm up a new domain

Mailbox providers trust a domain that sends steadily more than one that appears overnight with a large volume. On a domain that has never sent campaigns:

| Stage               | What to send                                                                         |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| First week          | 50 to 100 emails a day, to your most engaged contacts                                |
| Weeks two and three | Roughly double the volume every few days, as long as bounces and complaints stay low |
| Weeks four to six   | Reach your normal volume, then keep it steady from one send to the next              |

Split a large list into smaller lists to send in stages. Each campaign can reach up to 10,000 people.

## Laws to know

This is general guidance, not legal advice.

- **United States (CAN-SPAM)**: include a postal address, a working unsubscribe link and an honest subject line. Opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days. Twenty processes them immediately.
- **European Union and United Kingdom (GDPR, ePrivacy, PECR)**: marketing email generally needs the person's prior consent, and you must be able to show when and how you got it.
- **Canada (CASL)**: you need express or implied consent before sending, and must be able to prove it.
