---
title: Set Up a Sending Domain
description: Verify your domain so Twenty can send campaigns from your own address.
---

Campaigns go out from an address on your own domain, like `news@yourcompany.com`. Before Twenty can send from it, mailbox providers such as Gmail and Outlook need proof that the domain allows it. You give that proof by adding a few DNS records at the company that manages your domain.

Setup has three steps: create the address in Twenty, add the records at your DNS provider, then let Twenty verify them.

<Note>
  Twenty's records sit on their own host names, such as
  `twenty-bounce.yourcompany.com` and `unsubscribe.yourcompany.com`. They do not
  change the records your everyday email relies on, so you can safely use your
  main domain. The only exception is the DMARC record, which applies to the
  whole domain, and which Twenty suggests only when your domain has none.
</Note>

## Before you start

You need two things:

- **Access to your DNS provider**: the company where you manage your domain's records, for example Cloudflare, GoDaddy or Namecheap. This is often, but not always, the company you bought the domain from.
- **The Workspace settings permission** in Twenty.

## Part 1: Create the address in Twenty

The sending domain comes from an email channel. If you already set up a shared inbox in Twenty, the domain is already there, and you can skip to Part 2.

1. Go to **Settings → Communication**.
2. Click **Add email channel**.

<img
  src="/images/user-guide/emails/inbound-channels/03-twenty-add-email-channel.png"
  style={{ width: '100%' }}
/>

3. In **Source Email Address**, type the address you want campaigns to come from, for example `news@yourcompany.com`. Twenty uses everything after the `@` as the sending domain.
4. In **Display Name**, type the name recipients see in their inbox, for example `Acme Newsletter`.
5. Click **Save**.

<img
  src="/images/user-guide/emails/inbound-channels/04-twenty-new-channel-form.png"
  style={{ width: '100%' }}
/>

Twenty opens the channel. Scroll to the **Sending domain** section. It lists the records to add, grouped by what they do, with a copy button next to each value.

<Note>
  A domain can only be verified in one Twenty workspace. If another workspace
  already uses it, Twenty tells you when you save.
</Note>

## Part 2: Understand the records

| Group                     | Records                                                                                           | What it does                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Domain authentication** | Three `CNAME` records ending in `._domainkey`, plus an `MX` and a `TXT` record on `twenty-bounce` | Proves your domain allows Twenty to send for it (DKIM and SPF). The `twenty-bounce` records also route bounce reports back to Twenty.      |
| **DMARC policy**          | One `TXT` record on `_dmarc`                                                                      | Tells mailbox providers what to do with mail that fails the checks. Twenty only lists it if your domain has no DMARC record yet.           |
| **Unsubscribe link**      | Two `CNAME` records, on `unsubscribe` and `_acme-challenge.unsubscribe`                           | Hosts the unsubscribe page on your own domain, for example `unsubscribe.yourcompany.com`. Campaigns cannot be sent until this is verified. |

Add every record Twenty shows. Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook reject bulk mail that is not authenticated, so skipping one costs you inbox placement rather than saving time.

<Warning>
  If your domain already has a DMARC record, keep it and do not add a second
  one. A domain with two DMARC records is treated as having none.
</Warning>

## Part 3: Add the records at your DNS provider

For each record, create a new entry at your DNS provider and copy three values from Twenty:

- **Type**: `CNAME`, `MX` or `TXT`.
- **Name**: sometimes called **Host**.
- **Value**: sometimes called **Target**, **Points to**, **Content** or **Answer**.

For the `MX` record, Twenty shows the priority in front of the value, as in `10 feedback-smtp.eu-central-1.amazonses.com`. Most providers have a separate **Priority** field: put `10` there and the rest in the value.

<Warning>
  Many providers add your domain to the end of the **Name** automatically. If
  yours does, paste only the part before your domain. For
  `twenty-bounce.yourcompany.com`, enter `twenty-bounce`. Otherwise the record
  ends up at `twenty-bounce.yourcompany.com.yourcompany.com` and never verifies.
</Warning>

## Part 4: Verify

Twenty checks your records every hour on its own. To check right away, click **Check verification** in the **Sending domain** section.

DNS changes usually take effect within an hour, but some providers take up to 48 hours. The domain moves from **Pending** to **Verified** once the authentication records are found, and the **Unsubscribe link** row turns **Verified** once its records are found. You can send campaigns when both are verified.

The **DMARC policy** row always shows **Not set**. This is expected: Twenty suggests the record but does not check it.

## Troubleshooting

<Accordion title="The domain stays Pending for hours">
  Open your DNS provider and compare each **Name** with the one in Twenty. The
  most common cause is the provider adding your domain a second time, as
  described in Part 3. Also check that the records were saved at the provider
  that actually hosts your DNS. If your domain's name servers point elsewhere,
  records added in your registrar's panel have no effect.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Cloudflare: the records are there but never verify">
  Each `CNAME` must be set to **DNS only**. With the orange cloud on, Cloudflare
  answers with its own servers instead of the value Twenty gave you.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The domain shows Failed">
  Twenty stops checking a failed domain on its own. Fix the records, then click
  **Check verification**.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The domain shows Temporary Failure">
  Your DNS provider did not answer when Twenty checked. Twenty keeps checking
  every hour, so this usually resolves on its own.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Authentication is verified but the Unsubscribe link stays Pending">
  The two unsubscribe records are checked separately and can take a little
  longer, because Twenty also issues a secure certificate for them. Check that
  both `CNAME` records exist, on `unsubscribe` and
  `_acme-challenge.unsubscribe`, then wait up to an hour.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="My provider rejects the MX record">
  Put `10` in the **Priority** field and only the host name in the value, as in
  `feedback-smtp.eu-central-1.amazonses.com`. Do not include the `10` in the
  value itself.
</Accordion>
